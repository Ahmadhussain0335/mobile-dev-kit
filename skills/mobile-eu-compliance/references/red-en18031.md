# Radio Equipment Directive delegated act and EN 18031

Load this when a Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular or other radio device is placed on the EU market and the app
pairs with it, controls it, updates it or handles its data. Engineering guidance, not legal advice.

What could be verified: the delegated act (EU) 2022/30, its amendment (EU) 2023/2444 and Implementing
Decision (EU) 2025/138 from the Official Journal. The EN 18031 standards are paid documents; clause numbers
below are only those quoted in the Implementing Decision. For any other clause, the agent must ask for the
team's copy of the standard instead of citing from memory.

## Dates

- Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 applies from 1 August 2025 (originally 1 August 2024, moved by
  Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2444).
- EN 18031-1:2024, EN 18031-2:2024 and EN 18031-3:2024 were cited, with restrictions, by Implementing
  Decision (EU) 2025/138 of 28 January 2025. Presumption of conformity starts from publication of the reference.
- The CRA recitals expect the Commission to repeal or amend Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 for products
  that the CRA covers. Until that happens, both apply to a radio device. Check the current status before
  planning beyond 2027.

## Which requirement applies (Art. 1 of 2022/30)

| RED requirement | Applies to | Harmonised standard |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 3(3)(d) no harm to the network, no misuse of network resources | Internet-connected radio equipment: equipment that "can communicate itself over the internet, whether it communicates directly or via any other equipment" | EN 18031-1 |
| Art. 3(3)(e) protection of personal data and privacy | If it can process personal data, traffic data or location data: internet-connected radio equipment, childcare equipment, toys (Directive 2009/48/EC), and equipment worn on the body or clothing | EN 18031-2 |
| Art. 3(3)(f) protection from fraud | Internet-connected radio equipment that lets the user transfer money, monetary value or virtual currency | EN 18031-3 |

Exclusions (Art. 2 of 2022/30):
- None of the three apply to equipment covered by the medical device regulations (EU) 2017/745 or 2017/746.
- (e) and (f) do not apply to equipment covered by (EU) 2018/1139 (civil aviation), (EU) 2019/2144 (vehicle
  type approval) or Directive (EU) 2019/520 (electronic road tolling).

### BLE devices that reach the internet through the phone

The text covers equipment that communicates over the internet "directly or via any other equipment". Whether
a BLE accessory whose data reaches a cloud only through the companion app falls under that phrase is not
settled by an official Commission statement that this pack could verify. Industry papers and law firms read
it differently. The agent records this as an open scope question, lists the facts (does the device originate
or receive internet traffic through the phone, for example firmware downloads or cloud commands relayed by
the app?), and does not decide it.

## Restrictions in Implementing Decision (EU) 2025/138

| Standard | Restriction |
|---|---|
| All three | The "rationale" and "guidance" sections do not confer a presumption of conformity |
| All three | No presumption of conformity if, when applying clauses 6.2.5.1 and 6.2.5.2, "the user is allowed not to set and use any password" |
| EN 18031-2 | For equipment covered by clauses 6.1.3 to 6.1.6 (toys and childcare), no presumption of conformity if, applying clauses 6.1.3.4.2, 6.1.4.4.2, 6.1.5.4.2 and 6.1.6.4.2, "parental or guardian access control is not ensured" |
| EN 18031-3 | The assessment criteria of clause 6.3.2.4 (secure updates) do not confer a presumption of conformity |

Where a restriction applies, the manufacturer cannot rely on the standard alone for that point; the
conformity route then needs a different justification or a notified body. That decision belongs to the
compliance owner.

## What the app side usually has to provide

The standards are written for the radio equipment, but a companion app often implements the device's
security functions. Map each to the device's mechanisms and to evidence in the app.

| Area | App-side capability | Evidence in an app codebase |
|---|---|---|
| Authentication, no default passwords | Pairing requires a per-device secret or a user-set password; no skip; brute-force protection | Onboarding flow, pairing code, lockout handling |
| Access control | Only the owner (and invited users) can control the device; roles enforced on device or backend | Device sharing flow, backend authorization checks |
| Secure update | App delivers firmware over TLS; image is signed and verified by the device; no downgrade to vulnerable versions | DFU code, signature verification on device, version checks |
| Secure storage | Device credentials and keys stored in Keystore / Keychain; never in logs or plain files | Storage layer, key handling |
| Secure communication | TLS to backend and brokers; BLE LE Secure Connections pairing plus app-layer authentication or encryption for sensitive commands | Network config, BLE protocol spec, command authentication |
| Resilience | Reconnect with backoff, no flooding of the device or network, safe behaviour on malformed input | Reconnect logic, input validation |
| Logging | Security events recorded without personal data | Event log design |
| Deletion | User can remove personal data from app, backend and device (factory reset) | Delete-account and reset flows |
| User notification | User is told about security-relevant changes, for example a new device owner or a failed update | Notification flows |

## ETSI EN 303 645 (consumer IoT baseline)

ETSI EN 303 645 V3.1.3 (published September 2024) is a widely used consumer IoT baseline; ETSI TS 103 701
V2.1.1 describes how to assess against it. It is not the harmonised standard for the RED delegated act, but
its 13 provisions overlap with EN 18031 and the CRA and are useful as a checklist:

1. No universal default passwords
2. Implement a means to manage reports of vulnerabilities
3. Keep software updated
4. Securely store sensitive security parameters
5. Communicate securely
6. Minimise exposed attack surfaces
7. Ensure software integrity
8. Ensure that personal data is secure
9. Make systems resilient to outages
10. Examine system telemetry data
11. Make it easy for users to delete user data
12. Make installation and maintenance of devices easy
13. Validate input data
